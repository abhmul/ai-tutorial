**Permission and security fatigue.** NIST. (2016). [Security Fatigue Can Cause Computer Users to Feel Hopeless and Act Recklessly](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2016/10/security-fatigue-can-cause-computer-users-feel-hopeless-and-act-recklessly). Stanton, B., Theofanos, M. F., Prettyman, S. S., & Furman, S. (2016). Security fatigue. *IT Professional*, 18(5), 26–32. DOI: [10.1109/MITP.2016.84](https://doi.org/10.1109/MITP.2016.84).

**Citation fabrication.** Walters, W. H., & Wilder, E. I. (2023). Fabrication and errors in the bibliographic citations generated by ChatGPT. *Scientific Reports*, 13:14045. DOI: [10.1038/s41598-023-41032-5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-41032-5) — GPT-3.5 fabricated 55% of citations, GPT-4 18%; 24–43% of the non-fabricated citations still had errors.

**Sycophancy.** Sharma et al. (2023). Towards Understanding Sycophancy in Language Models. arXiv:[2310.13548](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548) (Anthropic; published at ICLR 2024, peer-reviewed). — Leading assistants consistently exhibit sycophancy; humans and preference models sometimes favor convincing but incorrect answers.

**Confabulation.** Farquhar et al. (2024). Detecting hallucinations in large language models using semantic entropy. *Nature*, 630:625–630. DOI: [10.1038/s41586-024-07421-0](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07421-0) — Confident, arbitrary, incorrect generations that change across re-asking.
